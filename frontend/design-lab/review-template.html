01 · 10

The three active mechanics became one

The rejected decision was structural, not cosmetic. The sidebar had three different answers to “which page am I on”: a carved band when docked and expanded, an accent-tinted tile plus a 2px marker bar on the rail, and a translucent accent wash in the hover reveal. Collapsing the sidebar therefore did not narrow one object — it swapped in a different one that happened to list the same modules.

There is now one element, and it morphs. At rail width it is the icon's own 36×36 tile, fully round, standing clear of the trailing edge, with its fillets faded out because there is no edge to close around. Open, it is the band: flush with the shell's edge, pill on the leading side, with the two concave fillets closing the material back around it. Between the two it is the same span travelling and re-proportioning.

Two defects found and fixed on the way. The rail's carve rendered square-ended because rounded-s-pill asks for 9999px: when two radii along one edge exceed its length, CSS scales every corner of the box by the same factor, so a 36px tile got 36 / 1019 and its 20px trailing radius came out at 0.7px. Both radii are now half the measured height. Separately, the band lagged the shell's edge by ~30px mid-reveal: its width was a spring chasing a measurement that was itself a spring. The width is now a calc() over --shell-nav-w, the variable the panel animates, so it cannot lag by construction.

Motion

motion@13.1.1 was already installed; imports are from motion/react. It drives four things and nothing else: the panel's width (as a spring on --shell-nav-w, so the plate, the nav column, the footer and the carve all move from one animated value), the carve's vertical travel, the carve's shape parameter, and the nav label reveal. No dashboard card animates. prefers-reduced-motion is asserted rather than assumed — the carve was sampled across the frames after a navigation and showed 2 positions with the preference set against 19 without it.

02

The brand mark never leaves

The rail used to suppress the whole lockup on the reasoning that 56px cannot hold a wordmark. It cannot — but a wordmark is not the brand, and dropping the emblem with it left the product unidentified on screen for anyone who prefers a collapsed sidebar. Only the wordmark is conditional now, via a wordmark prop on Brand. The emblem is the same element in the same place in both states, so it transitions rather than unmounting and remounting.

03 · 04

The workspace stopped ending

The footnote is deleted, and so is the string behind it in both locales. But it was only the last item in a stack that read as a footer: the body closed 16px above the viewport, drew a rounded bottom edge and a border across the page, and left a strip of frame under it — and the shell's own plate closed above that with a 32px radius, lifting the sidebar off the bottom of the screen.

Three things changed and they are the same decision: the column lost its bottom margin (py-4 → pt-4), the body rounds and borders only its top, and the shell's plate is square. A rounded bottom corner is a statement that a surface has ended, and it has not.

The heading zone

The area behind Your supply at a glance measured #dee7f6 while the gutter three pixels away measured #dde2ea — a periwinkle butted against a grey-blue. They could not be reconciled by tuning, because they were two planes: the frame, and a separately-tuned “wash” painted inside the body to stand in for it.

There is one plane now. The body's fill is a ramp in the alpha channel — barely present at its top edge, fully opaque a screen down — so the colour behind the page heading is the frame itself, the same pixels the gutter shows. No blur, no backdrop-filter, no saturation trick: one linear-gradient. The wash tokens are deleted.

07

The search field speaks the same neutrals as its ground

The blue-grey was real and it was the ink, not the fill. The control took --fg-secondary — #5c6066, ten points bluer than it is red — and painted it on Quartz #f4f3f0 inside a #fdfcfb card, every one of which is warm. On a paragraph that mismatch is invisible; on a control whose entire visible content is one line of placeholder, it is the control.

A field-* family now derives the same neutrals the other way — Ink mixed into Quartz, so each step keeps the ground's temperature. --field-placeholder is Ink at 58% (4.5:1 on the card, so the prompt is real type); --field-hint is Ink at 45% for the icon and the shortcut badge, which must recede — it is an accelerator, and aria-keyshortcuts is what actually carries it.

Focus was a hard 2px band of --focus. It is now --field-focus: Lumen at 38% as a soft halo, with the border stepping up to accent so the field's own edge carries the state. No blue ring anywhere in the control.

08

Two shells, and the difference is saturation

Nothing is adopted. Option B lives in one CSS block keyed on data-shell-palette="ink", which no app code sets — only design-lab/palette-compare.mjs. Whichever wins, the other block is deleted. It is deliberately not wired to a cookie: a shell palette is not a user preference, and shipping the switch would make it one.

Option A's #001537 is hue 217°, saturation 100%. It was sampled off a rendered concept, which matches an image but cannot say what the colour is a shade of. Option B is built from two colours that are already the brand's:

The audible difference is saturation, not hue: 37% against 100%. That is the whole distinction between an ink and an enterprise navy, and it is why Lumen Gold reads as an accent on B rather than as a second primary competing with it. The frame is derived, not painted at low opacity — you cannot reach a light blue by adding a dark one, so the tint comes from Lapis lifted into air and mixed into a Quartz ground.

Contrast on #15222d, measured: --shell-fg 14.6:1, --shell-fg-secondary 10.3:1, --shell-fg-muted 4.6:1, Lumen 8.2:1. All clear.

09

Body fidelity: what closed, and what is still open

This pass took the composition-level half — the relationships that decide whether the modules read as one designed page: the frame plane, the heading band, the body's continuity, and the shell's edge. It did not re-cut the individual boards against dashboard.png.

Still open, in the order I would take them.
  • The page heading is oversized. The concept's Dashboard sits at ~32px over a single quiet line; ours runs much larger and pushes the KPI strip roughly 55px further down, which costs the fold its second board.
  • KPI tiles are taller than the concept's and their captions wrap. The concept pairs a tinted icon chip with a one-line delta (▲ 12% vs. last 30 days); ours carries a wrapping description line instead.
  • The attention board's filter row differs — the concept uses two compact selects (All types / Due date) on the header line; ours uses a row of count chips, which is a taller and looser treatment.
  • The sidebar's trailing edge is straight. The concept draws an organic gold-edged curve. We have the gold hairline and the flow lines, but not the curve — and a curved edge has to coexist with a carve travelling down it, which is a real design problem rather than a missing asset.
  • Density gaps that are seed data, not design — product thumbnails, and an incoming-demand list two rows tall against the concept's five. Worth not "fixing" in CSS.
